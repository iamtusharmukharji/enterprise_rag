# Data Classification Standard

## Public
Public information is approved for unrestricted external distribution. Examples include published product documentation, public marketing material, and approved press releases.

## Internal
Internal information is intended for employees and approved contractors. Examples include internal engineering guidelines, ordinary project documentation, and internal process descriptions.

Internal information should not be published externally without owner approval.

## Confidential
Confidential information could cause material business, customer, or contractual harm if disclosed without authorization. Examples include non-public customer information, commercial terms, source code, and internal financial forecasts.

Confidential information requires access control based on business need.

## Restricted
Restricted information requires the strongest controls because unauthorized disclosure could create severe legal, security, or safety consequences. Examples include production credentials, private cryptographic keys, highly sensitive personal data, and security investigation evidence.

Restricted information must be encrypted in transit and at rest and must be accessible only to explicitly authorized personnel.

## Handling
Employees must classify information based on its most sensitive content. A document containing both Internal and Confidential information should be treated as Confidential.

When classification is uncertain, use the higher sensitivity level until the data owner confirms the classification.

## Sharing
External sharing of Confidential or Restricted information requires explicit authorization from the data owner and any required legal or security review.

## Related Policies
Security Guidelines defines technical controls for protected information. Incident Management defines escalation for incidents involving security or data exposure.
